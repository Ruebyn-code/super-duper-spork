# Opstack

A stock ordering application with a searchable catalogue, supplier filters, editable quantities, and saved purchase requests. Prices are in AUD. The app starts with sample stock; submitting a request does not change inventory or place an order with a supplier.

## Run

Requires Node.js 22 or newer. Install the pinned dependencies before running the app or tests.

```sh
npm ci
npm start
```

Open port 3000 in your development environment. Set `PORT` to choose another port.

```sh
npm test
```

Requests are saved as individual JSON files in `data/` (ignored by Git). Totals are calculated by the server from catalogue prices. This is a local prototype with Head Office and ordering accounts. Supplier orders use configured email sending; saved stock counts provide ordering guidance. Request files contain contact details; keep the data directory private.

## Railway hosting

Follow [Move Opstack to Railway and keep your Mac data](docs/RAILWAY.md). The included Dockerfile runs the app with persistent storage at `/data`. Configure a Railway volume, `APP_ORIGIN` for HTTPS sign-in, and `/healthz` as the healthcheck. Start in `MIGRATION_MODE=1` while privately importing the whole existing data folder; turn it off after checking the import. Existing accounts and records are retained. Use one service replica. Local `npm start` continues to use `data/` unless `DATA_DIR` is set; the account recovery commands use the same directory.

The account dropdown supports outside-click dismissal, Escape and arrow-key navigation.

The heading and Close button stay visible while its fields scroll; Close, Escape or the backdrop return to the list, and dismissal is blocked during saving. Settings are grouped into **General**, **Trading hours**, **Sending**, **Managers** and **Devices** tabs according to your permissions. One **Save settings** button saves changes across the tabs. Switching tabs keeps your draft inputs. Validation opens the affected tab; failed saves retain remaining drafts and report any sections already saved so you can retry. The Locations and Users lists use compact rows and search/filter controls, keeping user email, invitation status, permissions and additional location chips available.

**Managers** in the location popup selects existing users at that store and an existing permission level, defaulting to Franchisee when available. The popup’s **Save settings** applies that level in the current company without reducing broader permissions or changing network roles. ## Import weekly sales from Excel

In **Sales → Weekly report**, select the store, then **Import spreadsheet** and choose its `.xlsx` weekly report workbook. The preview lists the dated weeks, total sales and Uber Eats/DoorDash fees. Confirm **Import weeks** to fill missing entries. Existing matching values are retained; a week with conflicting saved values is skipped. If a report changes after previewing, review the refreshed preview before confirming again. Uploading or previewing a workbook does not save reports.

This imports **sales and platform fees only**: Till sales, Cash, Eftpos, Petty cash, combined Uber Eats brands, DoorDash, and each platform’s fees, marketing and refunds. Dates come from the actual Monday–Sunday Excel date cells. Amounts are rounded to cents, blank values stay blank, and cash/refund credit adjustments retain their signs. Labour, Op Ex, R&O, Cost of goods, operating schedules and Daily sales entries stay unchanged. Complete imported daily totals become the projection inputs for their dates; other dates continue to use Daily sales. Existing financial permissions and the selected location control access. Excel formulas and external links are never executed; formula values must have been calculated and saved in Excel.

## Invoice scanner and weekly bundles

The circular **Invoice scanner** button stays collapsed in the bottom-right corner of **Home**. On phones it sits above the bottom navigation. Open it to **Take photo** or **Upload invoice**, or drag a file into the upload area. JPEG, PNG, WebP and PDF files up to 10 MB are supported, with up to 20 pages per PDF. Camera availability follows the phone/browser file picker. Supported HEIC camera photos are converted to JPEG when the browser can read them; use JPEG or PDF otherwise.

Photos and image-only PDF pages use bundled English text recognition. Photo scans keep more detail, deskew the page and use automatic layout detection. If a scan is weak, the reader checks sideways/upside-down orientations, tries isolating a light invoice page from its background and retries with adaptive contrast handling. Word positions help reconnect separate label and amount columns. Field detection supports abbreviated invoice labels, amounts on following lines, spaced dates and tax-inclusive totals; payment instructions, quantities and due dates are not treated as invoice numbers, totals or invoice dates. PDFs with readable embedded text are extracted directly. Recognition runs in the browser using the app's bundled files, without an OCR account, API key or external OCR service. The recognition libraries load only when scanning starts. Check the detected supplier, invoice number, date, subtotal, GST and total before saving; all fields remain editable. Missing values stay blank, and unreadable documents can be stored with manually entered details. Recognition accuracy depends on image quality. The original upload and a small preview are retained (converted JPEG for supported HEIC photos).

**Weekly bundles** group saved invoices by Monday–Sunday for the selected location. The invoice date initially selects its bundle; change the bundle week independently if needed. Any signed-in user at that location can reopen an invoice, edit its details, move it to another bundle, download the original, or delete it with confirmation. Repeated saves do not duplicate invoices, and uploading the same original twice is rejected. Concurrent edits require reopening the saved invoice. Other locations and companies cannot read these files without access to that location.

After scanning or uploading, **Price updates** automatically identifies the company supplier and matches existing items by unique SKU first, then unique name. Unknown or ambiguous items are left out; no stock items are created. The review shows **only differing prices**, with each item’s current → new price. Unchanged prices, unclear tax/discount columns, unsupported rate precision, credit notes and conflicting duplicate lines are skipped. Explicit per-weight/per-volume units are converted to the catalogue’s ordering pack when the units agree; unclear conversions are skipped.

Press **Confirm price updates** to save the invoice in its weekly bundle and apply the displayed prices at every location in the company. There are no manual item selectors, price-entry fields or extra checkboxes. If nothing needs updating, the button is **Save invoice**. Applying prices still requires **Manage stock** and **Manage invoices**; other uploaders can save the invoice for an authorised stock manager. An invoice date is required for price updates. Prices remain unchanged until confirmation. Concurrent price, pack or SKU changes refresh the comparison automatically for a fresh confirmation. Older invoices cannot replace prices from newer invoices. Confirmations and audit history are saved together and retries cannot apply updates twice. Existing orders, stock counts and other companies stay unchanged. Reopening an invoice shows its price-update history; deleting it does not undo confirmed prices.

Invoices are stored separately in **data/invoices/**. Their saved **Total**, including GST, supplies **Weekly report → Cost of goods** for the selected location and bundle week, replacing order amounts. No saved invoices means $0; a missing invoice total makes costs and profit incomplete. Invoice corrections, bundle moves and deletions update weekly figures automatically without rewriting saved expense entries. Sales, stock counts, placed orders, ordering budgets and projections keep their existing sources. See [Invoice expenses](docs/invoice-expenses.md). Keep the existing **data** folder when updating the app to retain every weekly bundle. Bundled recognizer versions, source archives and licenses are documented in **public/vendor/invoice/README.md**; no additional OCR package installation is needed to use the scanner.

## Rostering

**Settings → Award compliance** includes a native calculator based on the supplied **MA000003 consolidation through 1 July 2026**. No payroll provider is required. Head Office has `awards.manage` by default; custom permission levels can delegate it. Confirm the legal employer, pay period and location public holiday calendars before enabling it. All employee profiles and dated employment terms are now edited in **Users → edit user → Employment & award**, independently of app permission levels.

The calculator applies adult/junior rates, casual loading, evening/weekend/configured holiday penalties, daily and weekly/cycle overtime, part-time patterns/recorded variations, full-time published-roster boundaries, minimum payments and recorded eligible allowances. Rosters and timesheets show itemised pay details. Paid rest breaks remain paid; actual timestamps are preserved. Approved calculations are snapshotted, with warnings if later work or employment changes affect them. Existing flat-rate behaviour remains when native calculations are disabled.

This is a wage estimator, not complete payroll or a full compliance certificate. Public holiday calendars, written agreements and conditional work facts need employer review. Super/on-cost settings are separate estimates. Leave, special arrangements, NES/state obligations, payroll payments and lodgements need separate handling. See [the source map, setup and precise calculation scope](docs/awards/README.md).

When checks are enabled, publishing and timesheet approval use fresh server-side results. Warnings require a review note and are snapshotted with the applicable version IDs. Actual clocking, corrections and time entry remain available; award warnings never remove worked hours or invent break deductions. Keep **data/award-compliance.json** and **data/rostering.json** with the rest of the private data when updating or backing up.

Automatic updates require a maintained source. No provider is bundled and the application does not scrape Fair Work or claim a live Fair Work integration. An optional trusted server-side `OPSTACK_AWARD_FEED_URL` can return the Opstack rule format below, with an optional bearer token in `OPSTACK_AWARD_FEED_TOKEN`. The endpoint must use HTTPS without URL credentials; keep tokens in the server environment. Enabled roster/timesheet use polls at most once per 24 hours per company, and **Check for updates** performs an immediate check. Downloads are bounded to 512 KB, time out after 10 seconds, reject redirects and import all-or-nothing as deduplicated **drafts**, never automatically activating legal changes. Failures preserve reviewed versions and produce a review warning. A payroll provider’s API may require an adapter to this format; the URL is not a generic payroll or Fair Work API connector.

A rule import is a JSON object with `schemaVersion: 1`, `awardCode: "MA000003"`, `label`, `effectiveFrom`, `effectiveTo`, `sourceUrl` and a nonempty `rules` array. A feed may instead return `{ "versions": [ ... ] }`, up to 30 sets. Each rule requires `kind`, integer `value`, and `clause`; `employmentType` is `all`, `full-time`, `part-time` or `casual`, `classification` is `all`, `1`, `2` or `3`, and inclusive `minAge`/`maxAge` bands default to 0/120. Supported kinds are `minimum-engagement`, `daily-hours`, `weekly-hours`, `minimum-rest`, `unpaid-break` (all values in minutes), and `minimum-rate` (value in cents/hour). An `unpaid-break` rule also requires integer `afterMinutes`; it checks recorded unpaid duration when elapsed shift length exceeds that threshold. Every imported amount, threshold and exception must be verified against the actual source. Values in automated fixtures are deliberately fictional and must never be used as legal award data.

**Shift clock** opens automatically across signed-in pages when an assigned published shift starts, with a reminder at its scheduled finish. It is no longer a menu page. The floating clock button reopens it; reminders never clock someone in/out or end a break automatically. Add each person's **Phone number** in **Admin → Users → edit user**, including managers. Their clock PIN is the **last four digits**, with leading zeroes preserved. Every user's PIN must be different across the network: saving a number whose ending is already used is rejected without changing the existing account. Users with no phone number must have one entered before they can use the clock. Existing manual PINs are replaced when the phone is saved; passwords and business records stay intact. Phone/PIN changes follow the existing user-management hierarchy and location access rules.

The blue **Shift clock** has a digital Melbourne clock and numeric keypad. Enter a fresh staff PIN to reveal the actions for each stage, then choose an action. Before clock-in, only **Start shift** appears. While working, only outstanding required breaks and **Clock out** appear; on a break, **End break** and **Clock out** appear. After completing all required breaks, only Clock out remains. Each action returns to the keypad. Unlocking alone never records work: unlocks are private to that signed-in session, expire after five minutes, and match a published shift or the exact current timesheet revision. Concurrent actions, another sign-in, a changed phone or a later clock stage cannot reuse an earlier unlock. PINs are stored as salted scrypt hashes and never returned as separate API credentials. Phone numbers are visible to authorised user managers. Five incorrect PIN attempts cause a 15-minute limit that survives restarts; saving the user's phone again through Users resets their PIN access.

Staff can request an **early start any time on the shift’s scheduled Melbourne day**. After their own PIN and Start shift, a separate **Manager approval** popup accepts only the manager's PIN and identifies the approver automatically. Actual start time, scheduled start and approver are recorded; approval does not change award/overtime calculations. Clock-in requires an assigned published shift and respects approved leave.

The clock offers **paid 10-minute rest** and **unpaid 30–60-minute meal** breaks using Fast Food Industry Award clause 14/Table 2. Break buttons disappear after qualifying breaks have been recorded. The entitlement uses the greater of planned and actual worked hours, so extra work can add another required break. For nine or more worked hours, the default is two paid rests and one meal; a roster with at least 60 unpaid minutes uses one paid rest and two meals. A further meal becomes available if another five hours pass without one. Paid rests remain paid. Timers prompt staff to return and warn about short/long breaks, the first/last hour and combining rest/meal breaks. An actual timing exception can still be recorded with a reason for manager review. Managers remain responsible for arranging breaks and reviewing part-time agreements and actual award requirements.

## Learning centre

Validation opens the first field needing attention, and failed saves keep your entries with a message beside Save. The same Save action applies the location target level through its normal scoped settings. Unchanged sections are skipped; if one section fails, successful changes are retained and an explicit retry saves only the remaining changes. Inside **Item details → More actions**, select **Remove stock** to delete an item from the catalogue. Remove all a supplier’s stock before removing the supplier. Supplier and catalogue changes persist in `data/catalogue.json` across restarts. Keep the entire `data/` folder when replacing the application with a downloaded update.

Each stock item has a **Minimum order quantity** (default `1`) and an **Order quantity rule**, edited through its pencil. **Minimum only** raises positive quantities below the minimum; **Multiples of minimum** always rounds up to full order units. **Custom cutoff** uses a configurable whole-number remainder: with 15-unit orders and cutoff 5, 19 becomes 15 and 20 becomes 30. **Separate up/down cutoffs** lets you set each direction independently: with 15-unit orders, round down at 3 or less and round up at 5 or more, so 18 becomes 15, 19 stays 19 and 20 becomes 30. Leave either field blank to disable that direction; if both are entered, the down cutoff must be lower than the up cutoff. These cutoffs compare the extra quantity above each full order unit; orders still use whole units and retain the minimum. Existing single-cutoff rules continue to work. A zero cutoff always rounds a nonzero remainder up; a cutoff equal to the unit size always rounds the remainder down. No order is recommended when par minus count is zero or negative; missing counts or par remain unknown. Recommendations, catalogue additions and cart edits use the same rule, while par itself stays unchanged. Quantities and minimums refer to ordered units/packs: for kilogram quantities, use a one-kilogram stock unit and a price per kilogram. Company catalogues keep their own rules. Existing carts adjust to changed rules with a review notice; orders over the 999-per-item limit are blocked rather than truncated. Saved orders retain their placed quantities.

## Automatic weekly usage and target levels

Save a **Stock count** dated Sunday and place orders as usual. The app calculates each location's usage automatically:

**Sunday count + quantities ordered from Sunday through Saturday − next Sunday's count = weekly usage.**

All orders placed on the opening Sunday count toward that new cycle. Orders on the next Sunday count toward the following cycle. Order dates use Melbourne time, including daylight saving changes. Each saved order is counted once, even when checkout is retried. If several Sunday counts include the same item, its latest saved count is used; partial counts do not erase other items counted that Sunday. Zero counts are valid. Non-Sunday counts are available in count history and do not feed this calculation.

Open **Stock count → Weekly usage** for the automatic breakdown: Sunday stock, orders, total stock, next Sunday stock and stock used. Choose a Sunday-starting week to review historical or current calculations, search, or select a supplier. Calculations update from the saved counts and orders, including corrections; no separate weekly usage entry is needed.

**Order when level** is calculated automatically for each location from average weekly consumption in the last four completed Sunday–Saturday weeks. Open **Stock item settings → Ordering rules → Location stock levels** to see it; it is read-only and also appears beside stock/target details in Ordering and Stock count. With fewer complete usable weeks, it averages the available weeks. Incomplete or negative-usage cycles stay excluded, and no history shows **Usage needed** while existing manual-target recommendations remain available. A genuine zero-use average is zero.

A recommendation is made only when the available stock is **at or below** this automatic level, then tops up toward the target using the existing minimum/rounding rules. For example, average weekly use of 5.25 boxes gives an Order when level of 5.25 and a target of 6: 5.5 boxes recommends zero, 5.25 boxes recommends 1, and 4.25 boxes recommends 2. Fractional averages use the greatest three-decimal count at or below the mean. Sleeve counts convert to ordered units before comparison; Ordering also includes confirmed movements after the latest count. Existing cart quantities and explicit manual ordering are unchanged, and no supplier order is ever placed automatically.

Ordering cards and list rows show **Target level**, the location's average usage from the available completed Sunday–Saturday weeks within the previous four weeks, rounded up to whole units/packs. With one usable week, the target level uses that week; with two or three, it averages those weeks; with all four, it uses the normal four-week average. Two Sunday counts are enough for the first cycle; five consecutive Sunday counts provide four cycles. Missing item counts or a negative result leave that cycle incomplete and show what needs checking; they are not assumed to be zero. The current partial week and other locations' history are excluded. Hover over the target level panel for the exact average. The four-week window rolls forward automatically. With zero usable weeks, Head Office can open **Suppliers & stock → Stock items**, edit an item and open **Ordering rules → Manual target level**, then select **Save changes** for the selected location. Enter a whole quantity including zero; blank removes it. Manual target level is shared within that location and is automatically superseded as soon as a completed usage week exists, including a week of zero usage. The saved value remains a fallback if all four weeks later lack usable history. Manual values persist in **data/location-par/**; include this folder when updating. Target level values do not automatically add items to the cart.

Saved counts and order files remain the source of truth. Previous manually entered weekly usage files and browser drafts are retained but are not used by the automatic calculation. Keep the entire **data** folder when updating.


Ordering items show **Target level**, **Stock count**, and **Recommended order** together in both Menu and List views. The count is taken from the assigned location's most recently saved stock take, including stock takes on days other than Sunday. Its date appears above the catalogue. Items omitted from that latest stock take show **Not counted** rather than borrowing a value from an older take.

**Recommended order = 0 above the automatic Order when level; otherwise max(0, target level − stock count)**, using the saved whole-pack minimum/rounding rules. A zero count is valid; a missing count, or no usage history and no manual target level, leaves the recommendation unavailable. Recommendations use the counted stock directly. You choose the quantity to add to the cart. Saving a newer count updates the displayed counts and recommendations automatically without overwriting entered quantities.

Directly below the table, a single row of secondary-colour overview cards shows recorded report sales, cash to deposit (the Cash row total), placed stock orders for that week, labour, operating expenses, R&O and weekly variance at the end. The Profit card sits immediately to the right of recorded weekly sales: **profit = report sales − placed stock orders − labour − operating expenses − R&O**. It updates as entries change, with green for profit, red for loss and yellow for exact break-even in cents. It remains neutral until sales and all cost entries are recorded (enter 0 for unused costs; R&O starts at 0 for new and older reports), or while stock prices are pending. Partial reports show the number of recorded sales days. The card row can scroll on smaller screens. Percentages use recorded report sales; pending stock prices leave the stock percentage unavailable. Two compact cards beside Weekly overview show Uber Eats in green and DoorDash in red, with their logos also shown on the matching report rows. Select either card to open its five-column fee table: Fees, Marketing, Refunds, Total fees and Expected pay. **Total fees = Fees + Marketing + Refunds; Expected pay = that platform’s weekly sales − Total fees.** Blanks remain unknown; enter 0 for unused fees. Fee edits update the preview immediately and save with Save weekly report. Fees and payouts are displayed separately and do not affect profit. The header stays the same height; on phones its cards scroll horizontally. Older app versions saving a report preserve its R&O and fee entries. Reports persist in **data/weekly-sales-reports/**, with separate browser drafts for each user, location and week and protection against concurrent edits. These reports do not rewrite Daily sales entries or Home totals. For each historical source day, projections use the saved Weekly report total once Till sales, Uber Eats and DoorDash are all entered; until then they use Daily sales. Cash, Eftpos, petty cash, fees and costs do not affect that total.

The weekly report has its own **left-hand expense menu**: Overview, Weekly report, Cost of goods, Labour, Op Ex and R&O. It shows one page at a time and keeps the selected week, without repeated page headings. Overview is the first option in this same sidebar. The expense sidebar starts as an icon rail, expands smoothly on hover or keyboard focus, and collapses when the pointer or focus leaves. Its expansion overlays the content so tables and cards do not move. All expense pages use compact cards: four per row on desktop, two on tablets and one on phones. Cost of goods lists saved invoices, their GST-inclusive totals and links to open them, alongside confirmed cost transfers. It updates automatically when invoices are saved, corrected, moved or deleted. Invoice details require Invoice viewing permission; finance viewers can still see the aggregate expense. Labour calculates **hours × hourly rate**, allowing three decimal places for hours and rounding each line to cents. R&O uses named **quantity × unit cost** cards. Add, edit or remove entries, or choose Record no cost. Existing totals appear as an editable Previously recorded total card. Op Ex uses the bill schedule below; its separate Additional costs section has been removed. Earlier one-off costs remain editable as regular cards and are included in totals. Save weekly report saves all report and expense details together, with location permissions, drafts and revision checks. Expense edits do not affect Daily sales, projections, budget or Home totals.

**Op Ex** uses a separate, shared schedule for each location. Permanent expense types start with Electricity, Gas, Water, Internet & phone, Insurance and Waste disposal; add or delete types as needed. Each bill has its own **total cost**, **Monday start week** and **number of weeks** (1–520). Use **Save expense** to save the bill immediately across all covered weeks, including weeks whose reports are already saved. **Save weekly report** also saves pending bills before saving the report. Editing a bill keeps its ID and original start week unless you explicitly change its start date. Overlapping bills for the same expense type are rejected; use a later start week or edit the existing bill. Different types can cover the same weeks.

For example, a two-week bill starting **5 October 2026** contributes half its cost to the reports for **5 October** and **12 October**, then stops on **18 October**. From the **19 October** report, Total cost is empty and the type is ready for another bill. Previous bills remain under **Saved bills**, where you can edit or delete them. Costs that do not divide evenly into cents give the extra cent to earlier weeks, so the allocation matches the total exactly. A new bill is a separate record and cannot restart an expired one by accident. Deleting an expense type keeps its saved bills and their dates; delete a saved bill explicitly to remove its costs.

Saved bills are authoritative. Old weekly report snapshots and browser drafts cannot reschedule them. New unsaved bill drafts restore only against the same schedule revision, and conflicting edits from another user or tab require reloading. Existing schedules are imported once from their source reports, without deleting or rewriting the original report files. The report's Op Ex total and profit use the saved allocations plus any previously recorded one-off costs, which appear in the same card grid. Operating expenses do not change Daily sales, projections, ordering budgets or Home totals.

**Sales → Weekly report → Overview** (at the top of the expense sidebar) displays saved weekly reports for the selected location in a compact table: Sales, Cost of goods %, Labour %, Op Ex %, R&O %, Profit $ and Profit %. Switch between **Quarter** and **Year to date**. Year to date groups the weekly rows under Q1–Q4 headings, followed by the overall Year to date average. Both views use narrow columns and compact spacing, while phones can scroll within the table. Previous/Next quarter and This quarter change the quarterly view; the period is kept in the URL. Quarter weeks belong to the quarter containing their Sunday, matching the full Monday–Sunday report (Q4 2026 starts 28 September–4 October and ends 21–27 December). Year to date includes sales days from 1 January through today, with no future sales dates. Invoice costs use the selected full weekly bundles, including partial boundary weeks; they are never prorated into daily estimates. The comparison row shows the previous quarter's weekly averages, or last year's year-to-date averages. Click a week to open that exact weekly report.

Overview averages include only completed calendar weeks with all seven sales days recorded. Unknown expenses and missing invoice totals remain blank and are excluded from the affected average. Sales and profit show weekly monetary averages; expense percentages average the weekly percentages. Profit % compares total profit to total sales for the same weeks with known profit. Positive profit is green, losses red and break-even yellow. Canonical Op Ex schedules apply to each week; Uber Eats/DoorDash fees remain separate from profit. The table fits larger screens and scrolls inside its card on phones, where navigation remains on the bottom. The Overview continues to show saved weekly reports; forecast inputs use complete report totals per day, with Daily sales as the fallback.

**Projected weekly sales = the average sales total over the four completed Monday–Sunday weeks before the current week. Ordering budget = projected weekly sales × 28%.** All seven days must be recorded in each of those four weeks before a projection is available. Each projected day's sales average the corresponding weekday from those weeks. For each source day, use **Till sales + Uber Eats + DoorDash** from the saved Weekly report when all three are recorded (zero counts); otherwise use that date’s Daily sales entry. This applies to ordering and roster forecasts without combining the two figures, rewriting either input or treating blank channels as zero. Clearing an incomplete report day restores its Daily sales fallback. Imported reports use the same rule. The Projection & budget view shows the source weeks and provides buttons to enter missing sales. Correcting a past week recalculates the projection. The current partial week and other locations' sales are excluded; weeks roll forward automatically. Monetary calculations use cents and round the projection and budget to the nearest cent.

**This week so far** appears in Sales and the cart, and compares entered daily sales with the forecast for those same weekdays, through today in Melbourne. It shows actual sales, expected sales, and the dollar and percentage difference (actual minus expected). Blank days and future days are excluded; zero is a recorded day. Earlier missing days are flagged. Current-week changes update the comparison as you type and are labelled unsaved until saved. Viewing another week keeps the current-week comparison on screen. The cart uses your location’s saved current-week sales and refreshes when sales are saved in another tab or when you return to the page. A projection requires four complete previous weeks; percentage difference is unavailable when expected sales are zero.

Sales projections, Home, Ordering and Cart show the ordering budget for the current Monday–Sunday week until Saturday, then switch to the upcoming week at 12:00 am Sunday in Melbourne. Costs include orders and net stock cost transfers allocated to the displayed week; actual order counts and financial reports keep their own dates. Ordering and cart pages subtract the current cart as well. Budget warnings do not prevent ordering. Orders with pending prices leave the remaining amount unavailable, rather than treating unknown prices as zero. Saved requests count toward committed spending regardless of the supplier email sending result. Retries of the same saved request are counted once.

Shared sales persist in **data/location-sales/** under the location's ID and survive restarts, location renames and user reassignments. Previous **data/sales/** files are retained as historical data for their original locations. Keep the entire **data** folder when updating. Labels, fonts, and colours use the existing App edit controls and app theme.

Choose **State or territory** under **Admin → Locations → Edit location**, then **Save settings**. All eight Australian states and territories are supported, with separate choices for each location. Public holidays fill automatically for past and future years (2000–2100), including movable Easter dates and substitute days; there is no annual date entry or API key. View the dates under **Settings → Award compliance → Public holidays**. Forecasts use these automatic dates without a payroll review. Reviewed location calendars take priority for that year, so existing local exceptions are preserved. To add regional or specially announced exceptions, review and save the location calendar there, keeping holiday names consistent between years. Native award costs still require an explicitly reviewed calendar; choosing a state does not certify payroll settings.

Automatic calendars use the bundled [date-holidays 3.37.0](https://github.com/commenthol/date-holidays) rules, with ISC-licensed code and [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/) calendar data; source rules and legislative references are included in the package. Calendar rules update with app releases. State dates can need regional exceptions or changes after special announcements.

A solid blue star beside a day marks a public holiday in Daily sales, the Weekly report, Home and the roster (including phone views). Hover over the star or use a screen reader to hear the holiday name; part-day holidays include their hours. These markers use the selected location’s calendar even when sales history is incomplete. Home’s sales-entry marker follows its trading date for overnight locations.

Holiday forecasts use the same named holiday in the previous calendar year, even when its date moves. The prior holiday’s saved revenue is adjusted by the total sales increase or decrease since that holiday: sales from the following day through the latest completed Melbourne day before the target holiday are compared with the same calendar dates one year earlier. Public holidays are included in those totals, while leap day is excluded to keep the dates comparable. For example, last year’s $2,000 holiday becomes $2,400 when comparable sales have risen 20%. The underlying ratio is used before rounding the result to cents. Weekly report revenue still takes precedence over Daily sales, including for last year’s holiday.

Only the holiday day changes; normal days keep the four-week calculation. Sales, ordering budgets and roster forecasts share this adjustment, with Sunday ordering still using the upcoming week’s holiday dates and the same completed-history cutoff. The projection shows the holiday, prior date and sales-change percentage. Missing sales, incomplete comparison sales, a zero comparison denominator, missing prior holiday dates or ambiguous holiday names retain the normal forecast with an explanation. Saved sales, payroll settings and historical financial reports are never rewritten.

## Shared sign-in

Everyone signs in at **/login** using the same Opstack branding. Older company sign-in links redirect to this page and do not select a company. Users enter their assigned company and location after sign-in, and can switch only to companies and locations they can access. Company names, logos, stock, permissions and saved data remain separate in the signed-in app.

Head Office with network access and appearance permission can open **Settings → App appearance → Opstack platform branding & shared sign-in**. Platform logos, fonts, colours, background and interface text are saved separately in **data/platform-appearance/**. Company appearance edits cannot change the shared sign-in. The main platform logo is the fallback for sign-in; its navigation wordmark is the default for companies without their own logos. Uploaded company logos take priority inside that company. Keep the entire **data** folder when updating.

### Weekly order cost allocation

Financial weeks run Monday–Sunday in Australia/Melbourne. Orders placed on Sunday are allocated to the following Monday’s cost week; Monday–Saturday orders stay in their current week. This applies to placed-order reference costs, weekly ordering budgets, Home quarter order costs/averages and detailed order Reporting, including exports and quarter/year boundaries. Weekly financial Cost of goods and profit use saved invoice totals in their selected bundle week instead; invoice dates do not apply the Sunday order rollover. Detailed order reports filter by cost date and show the actual order date separately. The rule also applies to existing placed orders when they are viewed; original order timestamps, supplier sending and Sunday–Saturday stock usage/par calculations stay unchanged. All-time placed-order totals are unchanged. At 12:00 am Sunday in Melbourne, the ordering projection and budget switch to the upcoming Monday–Sunday week. The cart subtracts its value and costs allocated to that upcoming week from that week’s budget. The displayed dates identify the budget period.

### Stock transfers between locations and companies

Open **Ordering → Stock transfers**, then **New transfer**. The sending location is the location selected in the app bar. Choose another accessible receiving location, add items and quantities (up to three decimal places), and choose **Stock quantities only** or **Transfer quantities and stock cost**. Locations in the same company use the same catalogue item and quantity. Transfers between companies explicitly match a receiving catalogue item and an equivalent receiving quantity; for example, four 1 kg units can become two 2 kg packs. A unique matching SKU is suggested, and the sender can correct it. Transfers never add or change catalogue items.

Assigned managers of the receiving location also see **Stock transfers to approve** near the top of Home when they have Home, View stock transfers and Receive stock transfers permissions. Select **Review transfer**, check the items/receiving quantities, then explicitly select **Approve receipt** after the goods arrive. It disappears after approval. This panel is limited to the location’s saved manager assignments; other supervisors and unassigned Head Office users do not see it. It works on both Home dashboard profiles without displaying prices or financial totals. Closing the review never confirms stock.

The receiving location selects **Receive stock** and confirms all listed items. Both locations see a permanent record of the sender, receiver, items, units, quantities, cost choice, notes and timestamps. An outgoing transfer can be cancelled until received. Confirmed transfers are immutable; quantities and financial adjustments apply once, including after refreshes, retries and restarts. The history has incoming/outgoing/completed filters, an expanding search, pagination and CSV export. Destination choices respect the user's accessible locations and company-specific transfer permissions; switch the app's location/company to receive there. Users who only have access to the receiving location can still confirm incoming stock.

Sent and cancelled transfers have no stock or financial effect. After confirmation, usage subtracts the quantity from the sender at the original sending time and adds the mapped quantity at the receiving time. Sunday–Saturday usage is **opening count + supplier orders + confirmed transfers in − confirmed transfers out − closing count**, so moving stock is not mistaken for consumption. Original physical count snapshots are preserved. Ordering recommendations include confirmed movements after the latest saved count; that count remains visible with the available quantity after transfers. Future counts already include earlier transfers and are not adjusted twice.

Quantities-only transfers leave finances unchanged. Cost transfers save the sending catalogue's unit price when sent, credit the sender and charge the recipient by the same rounded cent value after receipt, regardless of the receiving catalogue's price or pack size. Set a missing sending price in the stock editor or choose quantities only. Costs use the receipt's Monday–Sunday financial week; Sunday receipts move to the following Monday, consistent with order costs. Cost of goods shows supplier orders and separate transfer cards. Profit, budgets, quarter averages, Home expense percentages and detailed Reporting include net cost transfers, including credits; all-time placed-order totals and supplier-order counts remain unchanged. **Reporting → Stock transfers** and financial drilldowns/exports show the underlying adjustments. Company/location comparisons do not duplicate a transfer: credits and charges cancel across the full network.

Franchisees and Head Office receive **View stock transfers**, **Send stock transfers** and **Receive stock transfers** by default. Customise them independently in **Settings → Permission levels**. Customised existing roles retain their previous permissions. Keep **data/stock-transfers.json** with the rest of the private app data when backing up or updating. Supplier emails, SMS and ordering platforms are not contacted for internal transfers.

Ordering projections roll over at the **start of Sunday (12:00 am), Australia/Melbourne**, including daylight-saving changes and year boundaries. The upcoming week is still Monday–Sunday. On Sunday, estimates use the four sales weeks completed before the current unfinished week. At Monday midnight, the newly completed week enters that four-week window; missing days keep its forecast/budget unavailable until recorded. Current-week daily sales entry and actual-versus-projected comparisons continue to use the current Monday–Sunday week. Dates, spending, pending prices, net cost transfers and cart deductions follow the displayed ordering week, with automatic refresh within about a minute or on returning to the page.

Stock price changes from stock settings or confirmed invoice prices update the latest saved order containing that item at each location in the same company. History shows revised totals and the original prices for audit. Quantities, dates and already-sent messages stay unchanged; no email is resent. Order budgets and reports use the revised prices, while weekly financial expenses remain invoice-only. See [Order history](docs/order-history.md).
